How it works

      
Five steps between your voice and the result, all of them on your laptop.

      
He follows you word by word. The accurate recogniser starts transcribing at your first pause, while you are still talking, so the sentence is already understood the moment you stop.

Deadlines

        
He reads your calendar and Gmail, and won't let a due date slip.

        
Link Google Calendar (including Google Classroom) and Gmail once. Jarvis finds every due date, including the ones buried in emails like “last date for submission is 25/11”, and reminds you 3 days, 1 day, 6 hours and 1 hour before.

        
    
          
  • Announces new deadlines and new mail in your Primary inbox as they arrive
    • 
          
  • Warns 10 minutes before a calendar event
    • 
          
  • “I've submitted the DBMS lab” stops the reminders for it
    • 
          
  • Read-only. Nothing is sent, deleted or marked as read

Hands-free computer

        
Apps, windows, browser, volume, files. By voice, at once.

        
“Open Spotify.” “New tab.” “Go back.” “Scroll down.” “Volume to thirty.” “Take a screenshot.” “Close it.” Two commands in one breath work too: “open Notepad and set the volume to 20.”

        
    
          
  • Dictation into whatever window has focus
    • 
          
  • Mishears are forgiven: “open crow” still opens Chrome
    • 
          
  • “Again” repeats the last action, “what did you say?” the last reply
    • 
        

      

      
Screen
Reads what's on your screen
“What am I looking at?” He captures the screen, reads the text with the system's own offline OCR, and summarises it.

      
Memory
Remembers, reminds, takes notes
Timers, alarms and reminders that survive a restart. “Remember that my project is due on the 20th” stays remembered.

      
Learning
Gets faster the more you use him
A sentence that led to the same action twice becomes an instant shortcut. Say “that was wrong” and he unlearns it.

      
Protocols
One phrase, a whole routine
“Work protocol” opens your editor and browser and sets the volume. Focus, party, clean slate, lockdown and goodnight are built in. Write your own in the settings file.

      
Study
Coursera on autopilot
“Resume my course.” He plays each lecture and moves to the next, tracks your progress, and stops at graded items. He never answers or submits anything for you.

      
Presence
An orb, not a window
On Windows he floats on the desktop as a glowing sphere that listens, thinks and speaks, with live captions. Five colours. He steps aside for full-screen games. On a Mac he is a compact reactor panel.

Security grid

          
He also keeps watch.

          
While he waits for your voice, a set of monitors runs quietly in the background and speaks up when something looks wrong.

        

        

          
          
ALL CLEARFace, files, network, processes, USB and sign-ins, watched continuously on Windows.

        

        

          
“Shall I shut down the computer, sir? Yes or no.”

          
Risky actions (power, delete, force-kill, shell commands) always ask first. If Face guard sees a stranger at the keyboard, they are refused outright.

          
The security tools only ever point at your own machine and your own network.

Questions

      
Before you install.

    

    

      
Is it really offline?
Yes. Speech recognition, the voice and the language model all run on your computer, and they keep working with the network unplugged. The internet is used for the first-time model downloads, and afterwards only for things that are online by nature: weather, web search, YouTube, and Gmail or Calendar if you choose to link them.

      
Will it run on my laptop?
Voice control and the instant commands run on almost any recent laptop. The language model is the heavy part. With an NVIDIA GPU of 6 GB or more, or an Apple-silicon Mac, replies take one to two seconds. Without one, choose the lighter brain during setup; open-ended questions will be slower but commands stay instant.

      
Does he understand my accent?
He was built and tuned by a speaker of Indian English. “Hey Jarvis” is detected by sound rather than spelling, two recognisers cross-check each other, and jarvis train-wake teaches him how you say his name. For other languages, set whisper_language = "" in the settings and Whisper will detect what you speak.

      
Can he do something dangerous by mistake?
Anything risky asks for a spoken yes first: shutting down, deleting, force-killing a program, running a shell command. Deleted files go to the Recycle Bin or Trash, not into the void. Closing an app is done the polite way, so the app itself asks about unsaved work.

      
My computer warned me about the installer. Is that normal?
Yes. Jarvis is a free project without a paid code-signing certificate, so Windows and macOS treat the installer as an unknown app. The one-line install in PowerShell or Terminal avoids the warning. With the zip on Windows, choose More info and then Run anyway if a warning appears; on a Mac, right-click Install Jarvis.command and choose Open. Every line of the installer can be read on GitHub.

      
How do I remove it?
Run uninstall.bat on Windows or Uninstall Jarvis.command on a Mac, both in the installer zip. It removes the program and asks whether to also delete your settings, models and face data.

      
Is this an official Marvel product?
No. This is an independent, open-source fan project inspired by the assistant in the Iron Man films. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Marvel or Disney. It is free, for personal use.
